# Managing an Existing Account Management Operation

## Context

I joined an existing account management operation rather than building the team and processes from scratch.

My role is to manage the day-to-day execution of the existing system, coordinate the account management team, monitor operational performance and identify risks or dependencies that can affect delivery.

The team currently consists of 11 account managers.

Each account manager typically manages 3–4 accounts across 8 social media platforms.

A separate group of account managers focuses specifically on TikTok.

---

# Team Responsibilities

The account management team is responsible for:

- Daily publication of creator-generated video content
- Managing assigned social media accounts
- Maintaining publication schedules
- Reporting completed publications
- Monitoring account activity
- Communicating content shortages
- Correcting publication errors
- Maintaining the required publishing volume

---

# My Responsibilities

As Team Lead, I am responsible for:

- Coordinating 11 account managers
- Monitoring publication deadlines
- Monitoring publication status
- Controlling reporting
- Coordinating communication within the team
- Communicating operational issues to other teams
- Monitoring account problems
- Redistributing accounts when necessary
- Escalating problems outside the team's area of responsibility
- Supporting onboarding of new account managers

---

# Daily Operational Workflow

## 1. Reporting Check

I review the reporting system at the beginning of the working day.

The goal is to identify:

- Missing publications
- Incomplete activity
- Delays
- Reporting issues
- Accounts requiring attention

---

## 2. Content Availability

The account management team depends on the creator team for the required video volume.

When account managers do not have enough content, they communicate the shortage to me.

I consolidate the information and communicate the issue to the creator team lead.

This creates a clear separation of responsibilities:

**Creator team → content production**

**Account management team → content publication**

**Team Lead → coordination and escalation**

---

## 3. Publication Monitoring

I monitor whether the required content is being published according to the expected schedule.

When publication errors are identified, I communicate the issue to the responsible account manager and ensure that it is corrected.

---

## 4. Account Management

When an account becomes unstable or stops performing, the account may need to be transferred to another manager.

I coordinate the redistribution of accounts when necessary to maintain operational continuity.

---

# Operational Challenges

## TikTok Account Failures

Some TikTok accounts experienced significant performance problems.

In one situation, I identified the need for replacement accounts and found three team members who could take responsibility for creating and managing new TikTok accounts.

I provided the required new email accounts and coordinated the transition.

This allowed the team to continue working with replacement accounts instead of waiting for the problematic accounts to recover.

---

## Urgent Staffing

When an additional TikTok account manager was needed, I quickly identified a suitable team member and transferred responsibility for the account.

The new manager was able to maintain the account successfully.

This demonstrated the importance of maintaining enough operational flexibility to redistribute responsibility when account-level problems occur.

---

# Cross-Team Dependencies

A significant part of the account management workflow depends on the creator team.

Account managers cannot publish content that has not been produced.

When the creator team does not deliver the required number of videos, the account management team may become unable to complete the planned publication volume.

In these situations, my responsibility is not to manage the creator team's production process directly.

Instead, I:

1. Collect information about the shortage.
2. Identify the scale of the problem.
3. Communicate the issue to the responsible team lead.
4. Monitor the impact on account management.
5. Escalate when necessary.

This prevents responsibility boundaries between teams from becoming blurred.

---

# Documentation Improvement

During onboarding of three new account managers, I reviewed the existing instructions and identified that part of the documentation was difficult for newcomers to understand.

The original information was distributed across management messages and fragmented instructions.

I reorganized the information into clearer, more beginner-friendly documentation.

The objective was to make the process understandable without requiring new employees to reconstruct the workflow from previous conversations.

---

# Current Reporting System

The current operation uses a shared reporting table where account managers add links to published videos.

The system is functional but has limitations.

Because multiple people enter information into a shared document, human errors are possible.

For example, a manager accidentally added publication links to another manager's section.

The incident did not indicate a failure of the employee, but rather a limitation of a manual reporting structure.

---

# Identified Improvement Opportunities

Based on working with the existing system, several potential improvements have been identified.

## Reporting

Potential improvements:

- Separate data input from management reporting
- Reduce manual data entry
- Add validation rules
- Prevent accidental entry into another manager's section
- Automate status checks
- Create a clearer management dashboard

---

## Content Shortage Monitoring

Potential improvements:

- Earlier detection of missing content
- Standardized shortage reporting
- Automatic identification of accounts at risk
- Clear escalation rules
- Better visibility between creator and account management teams

---

## Onboarding

Potential improvements:

- Standardized onboarding documentation
- Step-by-step checklists
- Clear ownership of each task
- Reduced dependence on previous chat messages
- Centralized knowledge base

---

# Scaling Considerations

The current system can support the existing workload, but increasing the number of accounts or launching a similar new project would create additional pressure on manual processes.

If the operation were scaled, I would prioritize redesigning the reporting and monitoring system before simply increasing the amount of manual work.

Potential changes could include:

- More structured data collection
- Automated validation
- Automated reporting
- Clearer account ownership
- Standardized workflows
- Automated alerts for missing publications
- Improved management visibility

---

# Management Approach

My approach to managing an existing operation is based on three principles.

## 1. Maintain Stability

Before changing a process, make sure the existing operation continues to work.

---

## 2. Understand The Real Workflow

Do not redesign a process based only on documentation.

First observe how people actually work, where errors occur and where the real bottlenecks are.

---

## 3. Improve Based On Evidence

Process changes should be driven by recurring problems rather than isolated incidents.

The goal is not to change processes for the sake of change.

The goal is to remove recurring friction and make the system easier to scale.

---

# Key Lesson

Managing an existing operation is different from building one from scratch.

The first responsibility of a Team Lead is to understand the system, maintain stable execution and identify where the system creates unnecessary friction.

Only after understanding the real workflow should processes be redesigned.

A strong operational system should not depend on:

- One person's memory
- Constant manual communication
- Individual workarounds
- Previous chat history
- Excessive manual checking

The long-term goal is to make the workflow clear, predictable and scalable.
