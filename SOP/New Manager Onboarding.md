# New Account Manager Onboarding

A practical onboarding workflow for bringing new account managers into a high-volume social media publishing operation.

The objective is not simply to provide access and documentation.

A new manager should complete onboarding with:

- Clear understanding of the publishing workflow
- Working account access
- A validated account capable of receiving views
- Understanding of content selection rules
- Correct product SKU usage
- Access to the required content sources
- Understanding of reporting requirements
- A successfully completed and reviewed first publication

---

# Why The Process Was Created

The original onboarding information was fragmented across multiple management messages and conversations.

This created unnecessary complexity for new team members because they had to reconstruct the actual workflow from separate pieces of information.

I reorganized the existing information into a structured onboarding instruction and used AI to help:

- Consolidate fragmented information
- Improve structure
- Remove unnecessary repetition
- Rewrite unclear explanations
- Turn scattered messages into a step-by-step workflow

The final process and operational requirements were reviewed and adapted manually based on how the team actually works.

The principle:

**AI assists with structuring documentation.  
Human operational knowledge defines the process.**

# Step 1. Provide The Working Instructions

The onboarding process begins with documentation rather than account access.

The new manager receives the working instructions explaining:

- Publishing responsibilities
- Publication requirements
- Content handling rules
- Product SKU usage
- Reporting requirements
- Deadlines
- Quality expectations
- Common restrictions

The objective is to give the manager context before asking them to perform operational tasks.

Documentation should answer routine questions before they become repeated Team Lead questions.

---

# Step 2. Provide Required Access

After reviewing the instructions, provide the access required to begin account setup.

Depending on the assignment, this may include:

- Email accounts
- Platform credentials
- Required social media accounts
- Login information
- Other account-related access

Access should be provided only for the accounts and platforms relevant to the manager's assignment.

---

# Step 3. Create And Prepare The Account

When a new account is required, the manager creates and prepares the account according to the provided instructions.

The Team Lead remains available for questions and checks whether the setup has been completed correctly.

The account should not immediately be treated as production-ready.

It first needs to pass an initial validation.

---

# Step 4. Publish A Test Video

Before assigning the full publishing workflow, upload a test video.

The purpose of the test is to verify that the account is functioning normally.

Check:

- Whether the video was published correctly
- Whether the publication is visible
- Whether the account begins receiving views
- Whether there are obvious platform or account-level problems

This creates an operational checkpoint before additional work is assigned.

---

# Step 5. Validate The Account

If the test publication receives views and no critical account problems are identified, the account can move into the normal publishing workflow.

If the test publication receives zero views or another significant problem appears:

**Do not continue scaling activity on the account automatically.**

Instead:

1. Identify the problem.
2. Check whether the setup was completed correctly.
3. Determine whether another account or setup attempt is required.
4. Escalate the issue when necessary.
5. Continue onboarding only after a workable account is available.

### Principle

**Validate before scaling.**

It is more efficient to identify a non-working account during onboarding than after the manager has already built a publishing workflow around it.

---

# Step 6. Assign Product SKUs

After the account has been validated, provide the product SKUs associated with the manager's assigned account group.

Explain:

- Which SKUs belong to which account group
- Where the SKUs should be used
- Why incorrect SKU placement must be avoided

Product information should be assigned only after the manager has a working publishing environment.

---

# Step 7. Add The Manager To The Content Group

Add the new manager to the communication group where creator-generated content is provided for publication.

Do not assume that access to the group automatically explains how to work with it.

The Team Lead explains the content-selection workflow.

---

# Step 8. Explain How To Select Content

Show the manager:

- Where new videos appear
- Which videos belong to their accounts
- How to determine what should be published
- How to avoid publishing the wrong material
- How to track which content has already been used
- What to do when there is not enough content available
- When to communicate a content shortage

This step is important because content availability is a cross-team dependency.

The account manager is responsible for publishing available content correctly.

The account manager is not responsible for producing missing creator content.

---

# Step 9. Provide Reporting Access

After the manager successfully completes the initial publication workflow, provide access to the reporting system.

Explain:

- Where publication links should be entered
- Which section belongs to the manager
- Which information must be recorded
- Reporting deadlines
- How to avoid entering information into another manager's section
- What to do when a publication or reporting error occurs

The reporting workflow should be demonstrated rather than assumed to be self-explanatory.

---

# Step 10. Review Initial Publications

The Team Lead reviews the new manager's first publications.

Check:

- Correct account
- Correct content
- Correct product SKU
- Correct publication format
- Correct description
- Publication visibility
- Reporting accuracy

If an error is identified:

1. Explain what is incorrect.
2. Show the correct approach.
3. Ask the manager to correct the issue.
4. Verify the correction.

The objective is not simply to fix the individual publication.

The manager should understand how to avoid repeating the same mistake.

---

# Step 11. Transition To Independent Work

A manager can move into the normal workflow when they can independently:

- Access assigned accounts
- Select the correct content
- Publish according to the required schedule
- Use the correct product SKUs
- Check their own publications
- Complete reporting correctly
- Identify and communicate account problems
- Report content shortages when necessary

At this point, Team Lead involvement shifts from onboarding supervision to normal operational control.

# Common Onboarding Risks

## Giving Everything At Once

Providing accounts, content groups, SKUs and reporting access simultaneously can overload a new manager and make mistakes harder to identify.

### Better Approach

Introduce access in the order in which it becomes necessary.

---

## Skipping Account Validation

Starting full production before confirming that a new account works can waste time if the account receives zero views or has another platform-level problem.

### Better Approach

**Account → Test Publication → Validation → Full Workflow**

---

## Assuming Documentation Is Enough

Instructions cannot replace practical validation.

### Better Approach

Documentation

+

Explanation

+

Test Publication

+

Team Lead Review

---

## Explaining Everything Only In Chat

Repeated verbal explanations create dependency on the Team Lead and make onboarding inconsistent.

### Better Approach

Document the stable process and use direct communication for exceptions and clarification.

# Team Lead Role In Onboarding

The Team Lead is responsible for:

- Maintaining onboarding documentation
- Providing or coordinating required access
- Explaining the publishing workflow
- Explaining content selection
- Assigning product SKUs
- Providing access to content sources
- Providing reporting access
- Reviewing initial work
- Correcting misunderstandings
- Confirming readiness for independent work

The objective is not permanent supervision.

The objective is to reduce uncertainty until the manager can operate independently.

---

# Offboarding / Final Work Control

When an account manager finishes their work with the project, the Team Lead performs a final operational check.

This includes:

- Reviewing completed publications
- Verifying the final reporting period
- Calculating the total number of completed publications
- Resolving outstanding reporting discrepancies
- Preparing the final work-volume information
- Transferring the required data for payment calculation

This creates a clear operational endpoint for the manager's work and ensures that payment information is based on documented completed output.

---

# Process Improvement Principle

Onboarding documentation should evolve when real onboarding reveals recurring confusion.

The improvement loop is:

**New Manager Question**

↓

**Identify Recurring Confusion**

↓

**Update Documentation**

↓

**Use Updated Process During Next Onboarding**

↓

**Reduce Repeated Explanation**

Onboarding should become easier with every new manager rather than requiring the Team Lead to start from zero each time.

---

# Key Lesson

Good onboarding is not simply:

**Give access → Explain tasks → Start working**

A more reliable operational model is:

**Document → Provide Access → Test → Validate → Train → Review → Transition**

The objective is to catch problems early, reduce avoidable mistakes and create a repeatable path from new team member to independent execution.
