# Team Lead Daily Workflow

A practical workflow for coordinating an account management team responsible for high-volume social media publishing.

The workflow is based on the actual operating model of the account management team and focuses on publication control, reporting, cross-team dependencies and operational risk management.

---

## Operating Context

The account management operation covers multiple social media accounts across:

- TikTok
- Instagram
- Pinterest
- YouTube
- VK Video
- Дзен
- RuTube
- Likee

The team includes:

- Full-cycle account managers responsible for multiple platforms
- TikTok-focused account managers

Account managers are responsible for publishing provided content, preparing publication descriptions, using the correct product SKUs, checking publications and maintaining daily reporting.

The Team Lead coordinates execution, monitors reporting and publication status, manages operational issues and escalates risks that cannot be resolved within the account management team.

---

# Daily Workflow

## 1. Morning Reporting Review

The first operational priority is reviewing the previous day's reporting.

### Check

- Whether required publications were completed
- Whether publication links were added to reporting
- Whether dates and links are entered correctly
- Whether publications are missing
- Whether reporting contains obvious errors
- Whether any accounts require attention

The objective is not simply to check whether cells are filled.

The objective is to identify deviations that may affect the publication plan or final reporting.

---

## 2. Identify Missing Publications

When publications are missing, determine whether the issue belongs to the account management workflow or depends on another team.

Typical reasons may include:

- Missing creator content
- Account problems
- Publication errors
- Manager availability
- Technical platform issues

Responsibility should remain with the team that owns the underlying process.

---

## 3. Consolidate Content Shortages

The account management team depends on the creator team for video production.

Account managers communicate how much content they are missing for planned publications.

The Team Lead:

1. Collects shortage information from account managers.
2. Consolidates the information.
3. Identifies the scale of the shortage.
4. Communicates the shortage to the creator team lead.
5. Escalates the delivery risk when it threatens the publication plan or reporting deadline.

### Responsibility Boundary

Creator Team  
→ Produces required content

Account Managers  
→ Publish available content

Account Management Team Lead  
→ Consolidates shortages, communicates dependencies and escalates delivery risks

The Team Lead should not absorb responsibility for work owned by another department.

---

## 4. Publication Control

Monitor whether the team is maintaining the required publication schedule.

Check for:

- Missed publications
- Delayed publications
- Incorrect account placement
- Incorrect product SKUs
- Reporting discrepancies
- Other publication errors

When an error belongs to the account management workflow:

1. Identify the responsible manager.
2. Explain what needs to be corrected.
3. Confirm that the correction is completed.
4. Determine whether the error is isolated or recurring.

Recurring errors should trigger a process or documentation review rather than repeated manual correction.

---

## 5. Account Risk Monitoring

Account-level problems can affect the publication plan even when content and reporting processes are functioning correctly.

Pay particular attention to:

- Account restrictions
- Account access problems
- Technical platform issues
- TikTok publications receiving zero views
- Accounts that become unstable or unusable

When a problem is identified:

1. Assess its impact on the publication plan.
2. Determine whether the manager can resolve it independently.
3. Escalate technical or platform-level problems when necessary.
4. Redistribute responsibility or replace the account when operational continuity requires it.

The objective is not to investigate every platform issue indefinitely.

The objective is to prevent account-level failures from blocking the wider publishing operation.

---

## 6. Team Coordination During The Day

Remain available for operational communication and exceptions.

Typical issues include:

- Publication questions
- Reporting problems
- Account access issues
- Missing content
- Platform-specific problems
- Changes in account ownership
- Manager availability
- Unexpected publication errors

Routine issues should remain at the account-manager level whenever possible.

The Team Lead should primarily handle:

- Exceptions
- Dependencies
- Risks
- Escalations
- Recurring problems
- Decisions affecting multiple people or accounts

This prevents the Team Lead from becoming a manual support function for every routine task.

---

## 7. Reporting Control

Reporting provides visibility into whether the publishing operation is actually being executed.

Review reporting for:

- Completeness
- Missing publication links
- Incorrect entries
- Publication gaps
- Reporting inconsistencies

When necessary, consolidate reporting information for management.

### Reporting Principle

Reporting should make problems visible rather than create additional operational complexity.

Repeated reporting errors may indicate a process-design problem rather than an individual employee problem.

---

## 8. Handle Manager Availability

If an account manager cannot complete the planned work, assess the impact on the publication schedule.

Depending on the situation:

- Confirm when the missed volume will be completed
- Redistribute responsibility when necessary
- Coordinate publication on another day
- Escalate if the absence creates a meaningful delivery risk

The goal is continuity of the publication plan rather than identical workload distribution at all times.

---

## 9. Escalation

Escalate when the problem:

- Cannot be resolved within the account management team
- Depends on another department
- Threatens the publication plan
- Affects multiple accounts
- Creates a reporting risk
- Requires management authority
- Requires resources outside the team's control

A useful escalation should communicate:

**Problem**  
What happened?

**Impact**  
What does it affect?

**Scale**  
How many accounts, publications or managers are affected?

**Owner / Dependency**  
Which team or process controls the underlying issue?

**Required Action**  
What needs to happen next?

Escalation should create visibility and ownership, not simply transfer frustration.

---

# Recurring Problem Rule

When the same operational problem appears repeatedly, ask:

1. Is responsibility clear?
2. Is the process documented?
3. Is the reporting structure contributing to the problem?
4. Can the task be standardized?
5. Can manual work be reduced?
6. Can validation prevent the error?
7. Does the issue belong to another team's process?

Recurring problems should eventually become:

Problem  
↓  
Root Cause  
↓  
Process Change / Documentation / Escalation  
↓  
Reduced Repetition

---

# Management Principles

## Control Outcomes, Not Every Action

The Team Lead needs visibility into whether the required work is completed correctly and on time.

This does not require micromanaging every publication.

---

## Keep Responsibility Boundaries Clear

Cross-team dependencies should not silently become account management responsibilities.

When the creator team owns content production, the account management team's responsibility is to communicate shortages and their operational impact.

---

## Escalate Risks Early

A known dependency should be communicated before it becomes a missed final deadline whenever possible.

---

## Correct Errors, Then Improve The Process

An isolated error requires correction.

A recurring error may require:

- Better documentation
- Validation
- A checklist
- Reporting redesign
- Clearer ownership
- Process automation

---

## Maintain Operational Continuity

When individual accounts or assignments fail, the priority is maintaining the wider publishing operation.

Redistribution and replacement are operational tools, not exceptional failures.

---

# Daily Control Checklist

### Reporting

- [ ] Previous-day reporting reviewed
- [ ] Missing publication links identified
- [ ] Reporting errors identified
- [ ] Required corrections communicated

### Publications

- [ ] Missing publications identified
- [ ] Publication errors requiring action identified
- [ ] Significant delays identified

### Content Dependencies

- [ ] Content shortages received from account managers
- [ ] Shortages consolidated
- [ ] Creator team lead informed when required
- [ ] Delivery risks escalated when necessary

### Accounts

- [ ] Critical account problems identified
- [ ] TikTok zero-view issues surfaced
- [ ] Account redistribution considered where necessary

### Team

- [ ] Operational questions requiring Team Lead involvement handled
- [ ] Availability issues affecting delivery addressed
- [ ] Responsibilities redistributed when necessary

### Risks

- [ ] Cross-team dependencies identified
- [ ] Important risks communicated
- [ ] Recurring problems documented for future process improvement

---

# Key Principle

The Team Lead's job is not to personally fix every publication, account or dependency.

The role is to maintain visibility over execution, coordinate the team, protect the publication plan, keep ownership clear and escalate problems to the people who can actually resolve them.

A scalable operation should gradually depend less on:

- Manual checking
- Repeated explanations
- Individual memory
- Chat history
- Informal workarounds

and more on:

- Clear ownership
- Useful reporting
- Documentation
- Standardized workflows
- Early risk visibility
- Appropriate automation
