# Decision Making Framework

## Purpose

A practical framework for making operational decisions when there are multiple priorities, limited resources and incomplete information.

---

## 1. Define The Decision

Before acting, clearly define:

- What needs to be decided?
- Why does it matter?
- What happens if no decision is made?
- What is the desired outcome?

Avoid solving a problem before understanding what the actual decision is.

---

## 2. Collect The Relevant Information

Identify the information required to make the decision.

Focus on:

- Current situation
- Available resources
- Deadlines
- Dependencies
- Risks
- Expected impact
- Existing constraints

Do not collect information indefinitely.

The goal is to have enough information to make a reasonable decision.

---

## 3. Identify Constraints

Consider:

- Team capacity
- Time
- Budget
- Technical limitations
- Existing processes
- Business priorities
- Quality requirements

A theoretically perfect solution is useless if the team cannot implement it.

---

## 4. Generate Options

Create several possible approaches.

For each option consider:

- Expected impact
- Implementation effort
- Risks
- Dependencies
- Time required
- Scalability

Avoid evaluating only the first solution that comes to mind.

---

## 5. Prioritize

When resources are limited, prioritize based on:

1. Business impact
2. Urgency
3. Risk
4. Effort
5. Scalability

High-impact, low-effort improvements should usually be considered first.

---

## 6. Make The Decision

Choose the option that provides the best balance between:

- Impact
- Feasibility
- Speed
- Risk

Document the reasoning when the decision affects other people or processes.

---

## 7. Assign Ownership

Every decision that requires execution should have:

- Clear owner
- Expected result
- Deadline
- Required resources
- Quality criteria

A decision without ownership is only a plan.

---

## 8. Monitor The Result

After implementation:

- Check whether the expected result was achieved.
- Compare actual results with expectations.
- Identify unexpected problems.
- Decide whether to continue, modify or stop the approach.

---

## 9. Learn From The Decision

After important decisions, document:

- What worked
- What did not work
- What assumptions were wrong
- What should be changed next time

This turns individual decisions into organizational knowledge.

---

# Decision Principles

## Do Not Optimize For Certainty

Waiting for perfect information can be more expensive than making a reasonable decision and adjusting later.

---

## Separate Reversible And Irreversible Decisions

### Reversible

Can be changed relatively easily.

Examples:

- Testing a new workflow
- Changing task allocation
- Trying a new content format

Move quickly.

### Difficult To Reverse

Have significant long-term consequences.

Examples:

- Major process changes
- Structural team changes
- Large technical investments

Spend more time validating these decisions.

---

## Escalate When Necessary

A problem should be escalated when:

- The decision exceeds the team's authority.
- There is significant business or operational risk.
- Multiple departments need to coordinate.
- The decision requires resources outside the team's control.

Escalation should include:

1. Problem
2. Relevant information
3. Options
4. Recommended solution
5. Risks
6. Required decision

---

# Team Lead Perspective

The Team Lead does not need to personally make every decision.

A scalable management system should allow:

**Team member → routine decision**

**Team Lead → exceptions, priorities and risks**

**Leadership → strategic or cross-functional decisions**

The goal is to keep decisions at the lowest appropriate level while maintaining visibility and control.
