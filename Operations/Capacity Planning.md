# Capacity Planning

## Purpose

A framework for estimating team capacity, distributing workload and identifying when a process requires additional resources or automation.

---

## 1. Define The Workload

Identify the total amount of work that needs to be completed.

Consider:

- Number of tasks
- Number of items
- Required platforms
- Required quality checks
- Deadlines
- Recurring workload
- New incoming workload

---

## 2. Estimate Team Capacity

Calculate realistic team capacity based on:

- Number of available team members
- Average output per person
- Working days
- Time required per task
- Expected interruptions
- Quality control requirements

Do not assume that 100% of theoretical capacity is available for productive work.

---

## 3. Compare Workload With Capacity

### Capacity exceeds workload

The team has available capacity.

Possible actions:

- Work on backlog
- Improve processes
- Increase quality control
- Automate repetitive tasks
- Take on additional planned work

---

### Workload exceeds capacity

The team cannot sustainably complete everything within the required timeframe.

Possible actions:

- Reprioritize
- Reduce unnecessary work
- Adjust deadlines
- Redistribute workload
- Add temporary or permanent resources
- Automate repetitive operations

---

## 4. Account For Buffer

Do not plan the team at maximum theoretical capacity.

Reserve capacity for:

- Unexpected issues
- Urgent requests
- Corrections
- Meetings
- Communication
- Platform problems
- Rework

A plan with no buffer is fragile.

---

## 5. Distribute Workload

When assigning tasks consider:

- Individual capacity
- Experience
- Skill level
- Task complexity
- Existing workload
- Deadlines

Avoid distributing work equally when the tasks themselves are not equal.

The goal is balanced workload, not identical task counts.

---

## 6. Monitor Capacity

Capacity should be reviewed regularly.

Track:

- Planned workload
- Completed workload
- Actual completion rate
- Delays
- Overtime or overload
- Unused capacity
- Repeated bottlenecks

If actual capacity differs significantly from the estimate, update the planning assumptions.

---

## 7. Handle Sudden Volume Increases

When workload increases sharply:

### Step 1

Measure the additional volume.

### Step 2

Compare it with current capacity.

### Step 3

Identify the bottleneck.

### Step 4

Determine whether the increase is temporary or permanent.

### Step 5

Choose the appropriate response:

- Reprioritization
- Redistribution
- Temporary resources
- Additional hiring
- Automation
- Process redesign

---

## 8. Automation Threshold

When a task is:

- Highly repetitive
- High-volume
- Rule-based
- Time-consuming
- Prone to manual errors

consider automation.

Before requesting automation, document:

- Current workflow
- Volume
- Time spent
- Repetitive steps
- Expected time savings
- Required technical changes
- Risks

This makes the business case for automation clearer.

---

# Example

## Situation

A large seller needs thousands of product cards created within a short deadline.

### Initial Reaction

Increase the amount of manual work.

### Better Approach

1. Estimate the total volume.
2. Calculate current team capacity.
3. Identify repetitive steps.
4. Determine which steps can be automated.
5. Create a standardized data structure.
6. Coordinate with developers.
7. Use the content team for validation and exception handling.
8. Monitor throughput and quality.

### Result

Instead of simply increasing manual workload, the process is redesigned to handle the increased volume more efficiently.

---

# Capacity Planning Principle

When demand increases, do not automatically ask:

> "How can the team work faster?"
