# Prioritization Framework

## Purpose

A practical framework for deciding what the team should work on first when there are multiple tasks, limited resources and competing priorities.

---

## 1. Collect The Tasks

Before prioritizing, create a complete list of current tasks.

For each task identify:

- What needs to be done
- Who is responsible
- Deadline
- Expected result
- Dependencies
- Current status

Avoid prioritizing tasks based only on which request arrived first.

---

## 2. Identify The Priority

Evaluate each task by:

### Impact

How strongly can the task affect the project or business result?

### Urgency

How quickly does the task need to be completed?

### Risk

What happens if the task is delayed or not completed?

### Effort

How much team capacity is required?

### Dependencies

Are other people or processes blocked until this task is completed?

---

## 3. Priority Levels

### P0 — Critical

Immediate attention required.

Examples:

- Major operational failure
- Critical publication issue
- Problem affecting a large part of the project
- Deadline that cannot be moved

---

### P1 — High

Important tasks with significant impact.

Examples:

- High-priority client requests
- Work affecting key accounts
- Tasks with important upcoming deadlines
- Issues creating significant operational risk

---

### P2 — Normal

Important but not urgent work.

Examples:

- Planned content production
- Routine process improvements
- Documentation
- Regular optimization

---

### P3 — Low

Tasks that can be postponed without significant consequences.

Examples:

- Nice-to-have improvements
- Minor formatting changes
- Non-urgent experiments

---

## 4. Consider Business Impact

When two tasks have similar urgency, prioritize the one with greater potential impact.

Impact may come from:

- Number of users or accounts affected
- Revenue impact
- Client importance
- Operational risk
- Time saved
- Strategic importance

---

## 5. Consider Team Capacity

Prioritization must reflect actual available resources.

Check:

- Number of available team members
- Current workload
- Existing deadlines
- Required skills
- Dependencies
- Expected task duration

Do not create a priority list that the team physically cannot execute.

---

## 6. Protect The Critical Path

Some tasks block many other tasks.

Identify:

- What other work depends on this?
- Who is waiting for the result?
- What will be delayed if this task is not completed?

Tasks blocking multiple processes may deserve higher priority even when their direct business impact appears moderate.

---

## 7. Reprioritize When Conditions Change

Priorities are not permanent.

Reassess when:

- A new urgent task appears
- A deadline changes
- Team capacity changes
- A major problem occurs
- New information becomes available
- Business priorities change

The goal is not to follow the original plan at all costs.

The goal is to maintain the most valuable order of work.

---

# Practical Decision Rule

When choosing between two tasks, ask:

1. Which has greater impact?
2. Which is more urgent?
3. Which carries greater risk?
4. Which blocks more work?
5. Which requires more resources?
6. Can either task be postponed without meaningful consequences?

---

# Team Lead Principle

A Team Lead is responsible not only for making sure the team is busy.

The goal is to make sure the team is working on the **right things**.

High workload does not automatically mean high productivity.

Good prioritization protects the team from:

- Constant context switching
- Conflicting requests
- Unnecessary urgent work
- Overloading individual team members
- Spending time on low-impact tasks

---

# Example

## Situation

The team has:

- An urgent publication issue
- A large planned content batch
- A recurring reporting problem
- A useful but non-urgent process improvement

## Priority

1. Resolve the publication issue if it threatens the current plan.
2. Complete the work required to maintain the publication schedule.
3. Fix the recurring reporting problem.
4. Work on the process improvement when operational priorities are stable.

## Lesson

Urgent work may need to come first, but recurring problems should eventually be solved at the process level so they stop consuming urgent attention.
