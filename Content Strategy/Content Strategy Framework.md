# Content Strategy Framework

## Purpose

A practical framework for planning and evaluating content based on audience needs, relevance and business objectives.

The goal is to avoid producing content simply for the sake of maintaining volume.

Content should have a clear reason to exist.

---

# 1. Define The Objective

Before creating content, define what the content should achieve.

Possible objectives:

- Educate
- Increase awareness
- Generate interest
- Answer a common question
- Address a problem
- Support a product
- Build trust
- Drive an action

One piece of content does not need to achieve every objective simultaneously.

---

# 2. Identify The Audience Problem

Start with the audience rather than the product.

Ask:

- What problem does the audience experience?
- What question do they have?
- What confuses them?
- What are they worried about?
- What do they want to achieve?
- What situation are they currently experiencing?

A problem-oriented starting point often creates stronger relevance than starting with product features.

---

# 3. Evaluate Relevance

Prioritize topics based on how relevant they are to the audience.

### High relevance

- Current problems
- Seasonal situations
- Frequently asked questions
- Common misconceptions
- Practical everyday difficulties

### Lower relevance

- Generic information
- Broad lists of benefits
- Topics without a clear audience problem
- Content created only because the subject is available

---

# 4. Choose The Content Angle

The same topic can be presented from different angles.

Possible angles:

- Problem
- Question
- Myth
- Mistake
- Unexpected fact
- Comparison
- Step-by-step explanation
- Practical guide
- Checklist
- Story

The angle should support the objective of the content.

---

# 5. Choose The Format

The format should match the complexity of the idea.

## Single Post

Best for:

- One clear idea
- Short educational explanations
- Practical recommendations
- Product-related information

## Carousel

Best for:

- Step-by-step explanations
- Multiple connected points
- Myths
- Symptoms
- Comparisons
- Educational storytelling

## Short Video

Best for:

- Demonstrations
- Quick explanations
- Emotional hooks
- Trends
- Dynamic storytelling

---

# 6. Build The Structure

A simple educational structure:

1. Hook
2. Problem
3. Explanation
4. Practical information
5. Key takeaway
6. CTA when appropriate

The structure can be adapted depending on the format.

---

# 7. Develop The Hook

The first element should create a reason to continue.

Possible hook mechanisms:

- Unexpected statement
- Relevant problem
- Question
- Contradiction
- Common mistake
- Curiosity gap
- Strong observation

A good hook should be relevant to the actual content.

Avoid creating a dramatic hook that the rest of the content cannot support.

---

# 8. Deliver One Clear Idea

Avoid trying to solve every possible aspect of a topic in one piece of content.

A strong piece of content should leave the audience with a clear understanding of one main idea.

Additional information should support that idea rather than compete with it.

---

# 9. Add Practical Value

Educational content should answer:

> "What can the audience do with this information?"

Possible forms of practical value:

- A recommendation
- A checklist
- A simple explanation
- A mistake to avoid
- A next step
- A useful distinction

---

# 10. Develop The Visual Concept

The visual should support the content rather than simply decorate it.

Ask:

- What is the main idea?
- Can it be represented visually?
- What should the viewer notice first?
- Does the image create the right emotional context?
- Can the concept work without text?

For social media, simple and clear visual storytelling is often more flexible than overloaded graphics.

---

# 11. Review The Content

Before publishing, evaluate:

### Relevance

Does this solve or address something meaningful for the audience?

### Clarity

Can the main idea be understood quickly?

### Structure

Does each section have a purpose?

### Practical value

Does the audience receive something useful?

### Visual consistency

Does the visual support the message?

### Accuracy

Are claims supported and appropriately worded?

---

# 12. Learn From Performance

Content strategy should evolve based on observed results.

Track patterns such as:

- Topics that generate engagement
- Formats that perform well
- Hooks that attract attention
- Questions audiences repeatedly respond to
- Seasonal topics
- Content that underperforms

The goal is not to treat every result as an isolated event.

Look for recurring patterns.

---

# Example

## Weak Approach

Topic:

> Benefits of magnesium

The content starts with a list of ingredient properties.

---

## Problem-Oriented Approach

Topic:

> Why do your legs cramp more often in summer?

The content starts with a problem that the audience may currently experience.

It can then explain possible contributing factors and provide practical information.

---

# Content Decision Framework

Before creating a piece of content, ask:

1. Who is this for?
2. What problem or question does it address?
3. Why is this relevant now?
4. What is the single main idea?
5. What format communicates the idea best?
6. What is the practical takeaway?
7. What should the audience do or understand after consuming it?
8. How will we evaluate whether the content worked?

---

# Core Principles

## Relevance Over Volume

Producing more content does not automatically make the content more valuable.

---

## Problems Over Features

Start with audience problems rather than product features whenever possible.

---

## Clarity Over Complexity

One strong idea is often more effective than many weak ones.

---

## Consistency Over Reinvention

Reusable structures and frameworks make content production more scalable.

---

## Iteration Over Perfection

The first version does not need to be perfect.

Content should be reviewed, learned from and improved over time.

---

# Key Lesson

Content strategy is not simply deciding what to publish.

It is a process of connecting:

**Audience problem → Content idea → Format → Message → Visual → Action → Learning**

The strongest content systems make this process repeatable without removing human judgment.
